TITLE
Herd Immunity and Learning Externalities in Markets of Shared Foundation Models

ABSTRACT
Firms that price, lend and rank with machine learning retrain on data their own models helped produce. When many of them fine-tune the same foundation model, one firm's retraining changes what every other firm learns from next, and no firm accounts for it. We model this as a market of retraining firms and show that it is stable exactly when one number, the feedback reproduction number, stays below one. That number depends on how many independent models sit under the market, not on how many firms there are: at full spillover, fifty firms built on one vendor's model behave like one learner fifty times larger. Three remedies follow. Retraining in smaller steps helps only below a critical level of crowding. Correcting for feedback works like vaccination, and the share of firms that must correct is the epidemiological coverage formula for an imperfect vaccine, including an efficacy below which no coverage is enough. A tax on aggressive adaptation closes the gap between private and social incentives, and without it every market of two or more firms over-adapts. Our one measured check reruns an order-flow market simulator from earlier work and reproduces its published amplification to every reported digit. Every other check confirms a formula against the model it came from, and is labeled that way.

KEYWORDS
learning externalities, algorithmic monoculture, foundation models, systemic risk, herd immunity, performative prediction, Pigouvian taxation
