# Supervision from public prices

## The problem this answers

Everything in Theorems 1 through 4 involves `R` and `s`, which no outsider
observes. Supervisors do not get to inspect firms' models, training sets, or
vendor contracts. A theory whose every variable is unobservable to the regulator
it addresses is a theory of a problem, not a theory a regulator can act on.

## The mechanism

Near the boundary the system tells on itself. As `m_N -> 1` the aligned
combination of firms' decisions exhibits critical slowing down:

```
   lag-1 autocorrelation of PC1 of public quotes  ->  1
   variance share of PC1                          ~   1/(1 - m_N)
```

So a supervisor can estimate distance-to-instability from public prices alone,
with no access to any firm's model, data, or code. The estimator sharpens
exactly as the market approaches the boundary, which is the opposite of the
usual situation where risk measurement degrades in the regime that matters.

## Scope, deliberately thin

**In the body:** one paragraph giving the estimator and the consistency claim.

**In the appendix:** the real-data panel, PC1 variance share and lag-1
autocorrelation of cross-sectional spread co-movement on the 212-CUSIP panel,

roughly `4.3` to `4.4x` from calm to crisis in the base calibration. Expected

**With a placebo** on Treasury and macro series, where no dealer-model channel
exists. Run it and report it whatever it shows.

**Framed as consistency evidence, never identification.** Spread co-movement is
contaminated by common macro shocks, and the data is public proxies, not
trade-level TRACE. The claim is only that the observable the theory says should
move, moves. Any stronger phrasing is indefensible and a referee from empirical
finance will say so.

**Journal material, not workshop material:** the consistency proof, the central
limit theorem, and the detection sample complexity.

## Relation to PEBSA

PEBSA (IJECS 13(12), 2024) is cited once, here, and for a real reason. It
belongs to the same broad question this section asks, which is what can be
inferred about the state of an economy from aggregate public signals no single
participant controls. The contrast is the point.

PEBSA's measurement channel is **exogenous**: sentiment is a signal about an
economy the observer stands outside of, and the accuracy of the inference does
not depend on the economy's own dynamics. The observable here is **endogenous**:
the public prices a supervisor reads are generated by the very adaptive agents
being monitored, so the measurement channel is part of the system, and the
signal gets *stronger* as the system nears instability.

One sentence in related work, framed as exogenous-signal against
endogenous-signal macro inference. Do not oversell the link. The value is the
contrast, not a shared method.
